# Airbnb-Pricing-Market-Share-Performance-Analysis

Project Overview

This project is a Power BI dashboard that analyzes Airbnb market performance across 10 major global cities. The aim of the project was to explore how Airbnb listings differ by city, room type, pricing, customer reviews, host verification, and rating performance.

The dashboard gives a clear view of Airbnb’s market concentration, customer review behavior, seasonal demand patterns, pricing differences, and city-level performance. It is designed to help users understand which cities dominate the Airbnb market, which room types are priced higher, and how customer ratings vary across locations.

Dataset
The analysis was built using Airbnb listings and reviews data.

The dataset includes:

279,712 listings
10 cities
182,024 hosts
144 property types
5.37M customer reviews

The main datasets used were:

Listings.csv
Reviews.csv
Listings_data_dictionary.csv
Reviews_data_dictionary.csv
Tools Used
Power BI for dashboard design and visualization
Power Query for data cleaning and transformation
DAX for calculated measures and KPIs
CSV datasets for listings, host, pricing, rating, and review analysis

Key Findings
1. Paris is the largest Airbnb market
Paris has the highest number of listings and reviews in the dataset. It has 64,690 listings and more than 1.2M reviews, making it the strongest city in terms of both supply and customer activity.

The top cities by listing count are:
Paris
New York
Sydney
Rome
Rio de Janeiro
Paris, New York, and Sydney together account for about 48.4% of all listings, showing that Airbnb supply is heavily concentrated in a few major cities.

2. Review activity is also concentrated in major tourist cities
Paris, Rome, and New York have the highest review volumes. This suggests that these cities are not only large in terms of listings, but also strong in customer activity.
Paris, New York, and Sydney together account for around 47.3% of all reviews. This supports the dashboard insight that a small group of cities drives a large share of Airbnb activity.

3. Hotel rooms and entire places are the highest-priced room types
The dashboard shows clear pricing differences between room types.
Average price by room type:
Hotel room: around $800
Entire place: around $673
Shared room: around $580
Private room: around $462
Hotel rooms are the most expensive category, while private rooms are the cheapest major room type. This shows that room type has a strong impact on pricing.

4. Mexico City and Rio de Janeiro have the strongest ratings
Mexico City has the highest average overall rating, followed by Rio de Janeiro and Cape Town. These cities perform well across rating categories such as accuracy, cleanliness, communication, location, and value.
The lowest-rated cities in the dashboard are Hong Kong and Istanbul.
This finding suggests that a city with fewer listings can still perform very well in customer satisfaction.

5. Cleanliness and value are the weakest rating areas
Across the rating categories, cleanliness and value received the lowest average scores compared with communication, location, and accuracy.
This is an important business insight because cleanliness and value for money directly affect customer satisfaction and repeat bookings.

6. Most reviewers only leave one review
The review frequency chart shows that most Airbnb customers reviewed only once.
Around 86.3% of reviewers left only one review, and around 98.6% of reviewers left three reviews or fewer.
There is also one extreme reviewer in the dataset who left 283 reviews. This could represent a very frequent traveler, but it may also be worth checking as a possible data-quality outlier.

7. Review activity shows seasonal patterns
The seasonality section of the dashboard shows that Paris and Rome dominate review share during the European summer period, especially from April to August.
New York shows stronger review activity toward November and December, which may reflect holiday-season travel.
This suggests that Airbnb demand is influenced by location and season.

8. Most hosts provide trust signals
The host trust section shows that most hosts have a profile picture and verified identity.
Around 71.9% of hosts/listings have both identity verification and a profile picture, while only a very small share have neither.
This suggests that most Airbnb hosts provide basic trust signals, which can help improve customer confidence.

9. Airbnb listing growth peaked around 2015
The new listings trend shows strong growth from 2011 to 2015. The highest number of new hosts/listings appeared around 2015.
After 2016, growth slowed. The dashboard also shows a sharp decline after 2019, which aligns with the COVID-19 period and its impact on travel and short-term rentals.

Conclusion

The analysis shows that Airbnb activity is highly concentrated in a few major cities, especially Paris, New York, and Sydney. Paris leads the market in both listings and reviews, while Rome and New York also show strong customer activity.

Room type plays an important role in pricing, with hotel rooms and entire places being the most expensive categories. Customer ratings are generally strong, but cleanliness and value are the weakest areas across cities.

The project also shows that most reviewers only leave one review, host trust signals are widely used, and Airbnb listing growth peaked around 2015 before declining sharply after 2019.

Overall, this dashboard provides a clear business view of Airbnb market performance, pricing patterns, customer behavior, and city-level competitiveness.
